For elected officials and department heads

Open County Portal is a complete public website for a county, city or special district. It is free, open-source software: there is no license fee, ever, and no vendor can take it away. This page is the short version of the guide for officials.

Why now

Accessibility. The Department of Justice's ADA Title II rule requires government websites and online services to meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA. The project's guide for officials describes the compliance dates under the April 2026 interim final rule as April 26, 2027 for governments of 50,000 people or more and April 26, 2028 for smaller governments and every special district; confirm them with your attorney. Many existing county websites and records systems don't meet the standard, and retrofitting them is often impractical. The portal was built to meet it from the start, and every release is checked automatically.

Residents expect to do business online, on a phone, at any hour.

Open data. Publishing public records data in standard formats is increasingly expected by residents, the press and other agencies, and it reduces routine records requests.

What it costs

ItemCost
The softwareFree. No license, subscription or per-transaction fee to the project.
A serverYour own hardware, or a cloud server; roughly $50 to $250 a month for most counties (a rough estimate; it depends on provider and the size of your records).
Staff timeYour network administrator: a few days to install and set up, then about an hour a week. Each office: a few hours of training and content review.
Moving recordsUsually staff time; sometimes a fee from your current records vendor to export your data.
Card paymentsYour payment processor's fees, as today.
Optional helpIndependent implementers can do the setup, migration and training for you, at whatever price you agree. Nothing requires one.

What happens to what you have now

Nothing, until you choose. The portal runs on its own server. Your current website and records system keep running, unchanged, while staff train and records are copied over. On go-live day the web address is pointed at the new portal, and it can be pointed back the same day. Every import from the old system can be undone, every update can be undone, and every design change can be undone.

Who controls it

You do. It runs on servers you control, with your data in a standard database you can export at any time. The source code is public under the GNU Affero General Public License: you may use, change and share it freely. No company can make the software proprietary, and no vendor can hold your data hostage.

Decisions that are yours, not IT's

  • Fees. Confirm every fee against your adopted fee schedule; the portal won't go live until each one is confirmed.
  • Card processing fee. Whether to charge one, and adopting it if state law requires.
  • Payment processor. Use your current one if an adapter exists for it, or choose one.
  • Data license. The terms under which the public may reuse your data.
  • Accessibility contact. Who residents contact about a barrier, and how quickly they'll get an answer.
  • Records online. Your state's rules on what may appear online; your clerk and attorney confirm the state pack's rules.

A typical timeline

Estimates for a mid-sized county with one administrator; yours will vary.

PhaseTime
Install, set up, train staff with sample data2 to 4 weeks
Move records, bills and old web pages; offices review content4 to 12 weeks, mostly waiting on exports and reviews
Accessibility testing, go-live checklist1 to 2 weeks
Go live, with the old site kept running in the background1 day

Procurement

The software itself is free, so there's nothing to purchase or bid. If you hire an implementer for setup, migration, training or support, that's an ordinary purchase of services under your procurement rules. Ask your purchasing agent or auditor.
